Unit 3

Summary Post:



Having read the work of my peers that review the same case study as myself several areas stood out that I had overlooked. For example, Singh (2025) points out that the issues surrounding the lack of accessibility features are due to intense project timeline structures and need to deliver a product to a client. This is an area I was aware of but did not take this into account when writing my own piece. Bashir (2025) also points out the impact that this mistake may have developers as he points out that the lack of accessibility led to increased support costs and reputational damage. For my own analysis, I did not think to include ideas around how this lack of features may impact the company as I review the case study from the discriminated party’s perspective only. This reflects how ethics standards in the case study (Horton, 2018) are important to have been adhered to as it crossed the ethical boundaries of both the law (Equality Act, 2010) and computer science societies (BCS, 2024) overall.

Seminar prep task: Data collection articles



Paper 1:
Vieriu, A. & Petrea, G. (2024) looks at how university student view AI technologies and its impact on their education uses a questionnaire to retrieve results and then uses quantitative analysis (frequency/percentage). I selected this paper as it aligns with my interests and ideas around AI use. The paper (Vieriu, 2024) outlines ideas I already thought that AI in education can be both highly beneficial while also in some cases impacting student abilities to critical think. I think the research method was appropriate in this case as it allowed for data to be gathered from a large number of people and it could be easily converted into numerical data. The quantitative analysis of frequencies and percentages is the standard, appropriate method for summarising survey data to address perception and usage rates of AI in a university setting. The data supported the claims in the article such as how AI can enhance learning for some. In terms of improvements could have been to track students’ opinions over time to see if opinions change and to see the potential impact AI may have on critical thinking.

Paper 2:
Fridgeirsson et al. (2023) looks at exploring and identifying the challenges associated with Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning technologies within the Research & Development of the telecom industry in Sweden and Finland. This article uses a case study to review the how and why of Ai adoption within the telecom field, the case study uses interviews and document analysis (Fridgeirsson et al. 2023). I think the approach was appropriate as it allowed for detailed exploration of the topic through the interviews, these interviews were then analysed based on the themes mentioned. The research was able to support the claims through use of direct quotes that had been analysed and compared. To improve the research a survey could have been conducted to allow for mathematical analysis to be more easily completed on the data.

References:
Fridgeirsson, T. Ingason, H. Jonasson, H. Gunnarsdottir, H. (2023). ‘A Qualitative Study on Artificial Intelligence and Its Impact on the Project Schedule, Cost and Risk Management Knowledge Areas as Presented in PMBOK’. Applied Sciences, 13(19), 11081. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/app131911081
Vieriu, A. & Petrea, G. (2024). ‘The Impact of Artificial Intelligence (AI) on Students' Academic Development’. Education Sciences, 14(3), 343. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci15030343
