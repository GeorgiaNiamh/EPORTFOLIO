e-Portfolio Activity: Data Structures Reflection

The paper (Wang, 2023) is all about how students have a tough time getting their heads around data structure concepts, which are important in computer science. The authors are suggesting a new way to teach it: using stories and visuals to explain tricky ideas.
The article says that the usual teaching methods often don't help students understand the logic and abstract nature of data structures. To fix this, they want to use relatable stories – things from everyday life, history, or tech – to explain the concepts. Then, they want to make those stories visual with things like images, videos, and GIFs, to make it more fun and engaging.
The authors think that combining stories and visuals can help students connect with those abstract concepts and how they're structured. Ultimately, they believe this approach can help students understand better, get more out of learning, and improve their practical skills. I have applied the ideas in the paper to Instagram to help me understand it better.

Your Instagram Feed (like a list):

  • Imagine your feed as a simple list of posts. New stuff shows up at the top, and you scroll down to see older stuff. That's a linear structure – things are in a row.
  • Instagram needs to keep your feed in order, so it uses something like a list to store all the photos and videos.

    • Who Follows Who (like a connections map):

  • Instagram is all about connections such as who's following who. To keep track of all that, they use a "graph. "
  • Think of it as a map of friendships. Each person is a dot, and if you follow someone, there's a line between you. This helps Instagram suggest friends or show you relevant content.

    • Comments on a Post (like a family tree):

  • When you comment on a post and people reply, it can get complicated. The first comments, then replies to those, and so on.
  • That's like a "tree" structure – the main post is the trunk, and the comments branch out from it. Instagram uses this to keep comments organised and easier to follow.

    • References:

    B. Wang, X. Zhu, C. Shi, Y. Cheng. (2023) ‘Explainable Story and Visualization for Learning Data Structure Concepts’ 2023 5th International Conference on Computer Science and Technologies in Education (CSTE), Xi'an China, 21-23 April 2023, IEEE: pp. 326-329, Available at: doi: 10.1109/CSTE59648.2023.00063.
