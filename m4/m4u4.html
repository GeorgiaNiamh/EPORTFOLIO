Unit 4

Risks and risk mitigation Anton & Nucu (2020)

Main risks:

In the article Anton & Nucu (2020), say we don't have enough proof that Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) actually does what it's supposed to do. So, a risk is that companies are putting effort into something that might not be that helpful. This is obviously not a good use of resources for a company.
Anton & Nucu (2020) also mention that things like rules and what other companies are doing can push a company to use ERM. So, if those outside things change, it could mess with how ERM is used.
Risks categorised:

Every company is different: Anton & Nucu (2020) point out that what works for one company might not work for another. Things like the company's culture or big events like COVID-19 can change how risky things are.
Rich vs. poor countries:Anton & Nucu (2020) point out that most research is on rich countries. There's a risk we don't really know if ERM works the same way in poorer countries.
How do these fit into the traditional SDLC model?

The "problem areas" from the paper Anton & Nucu (2020) can cause issues in those steps:
Figuring out what you need: If the company doesn't know what it really needs for risk, the software might not have the right features.
Planning how to build it: If they don't plan for risks, the design could have weaknesses.
Building it: If the builders don't know the risks, they might make mistakes.
Testing it: If the tests don't check for risks, they might not find problems.
Giving it to the customer and keeping it working: Risks can cause problems after the software is released, too if it isn’t maintained properly.

References:

  • Anton, S. G., Nucu, A. E. A. (2020) ‘Enterprise Risk Management: A Literature Review and Agenda for Future Research’ , Journal of Risk and Financial Management, 13, 281. Available at: https://doi.org/10.3390/jrfm13110281

    • Discussion summary:


    In summary, for my original post I focused on the key ideas of not understanding a business and organisational problems as the main causes for the failure of projects based on the article (Agrawal et al. 2024).
    By reading the work of my colleagues I have thought about other areas that may cause project failures such as Gore’s (2025) mention of communication breakdown. This was an area I had not considered initially but I now feel it could be a key component for the failure of a project.
    Bourne (2025) also mention the idea of large-scale projects being difficult to manage especially when the need to work across different companies. This is an area I had not considered. The idea of misunderstanding across multiple organisations could contribute to the failure of a project. As if the planning phase is not robust it could result in projects being taken in different directions by different involved parties.
    Overall, I think that when it comes to the failure of a project it is rarely one lone problem that causes the failure it is often a combination of factors that result in the failure.
