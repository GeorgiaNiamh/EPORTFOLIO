Unit 10

Reflection on Software Quality


Verdugo et al. (2024) describe the 25-year journey of AQCLab, a laboratory that evaluates software quality using international standards like ISO/IEC 25000. Their focus is on two main quality characteristics: maintainability and functional suitability. The lab helps companies by offering third-party evaluations and official certifications, which prove that their software meets certain quality levels. Their approach is structured, based on carefully selected metrics, and involves both academic research and practical testing.
In contrast, Lenarduzzi et al. (2023) explore a more hands-on approach to software quality. They compare six widely used static analysis tools—such as SonarQube and CheckStyle—by testing them on 47 Java projects. These tools automatically check for problems in the source code, like bad practices or complex code. The study finds that these tools often disagree on what issues to report, which shows that relying on one tool may not give a full picture of software quality.
The key difference is in the purpose and depth of the evaluations. Verdugo et al. focus on thorough, formal evaluations that lead to certification. Their model is detailed, with multiple layers of metrics and a final quality score. On the other hand, Lenarduzzi et al. look at everyday tools developers use to check quality during development. These are quicker and easier to apply, but less consistent.
In short, Verdugo et al. aim for official quality assurance, while Lenarduzzi et al. focus on improving quality during development. Both approaches are valuable: one for formal certification and the other for practical, ongoing code improvement.

References:

  • Lenarduzzi, V., Lomio, F., Saarimaki, N., Lujan, S., Palomba, F. (2023). ‘A critical comparison on six static analysis tools: Detection, agreement, and precision’, Journal of Systems and Software, 198, 111575. Available at: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jss.2023.111575
  • Verdugo, J., Oviedo, J., Rodríguez, M., Piattini, M. (2024). ‘Connecting Research and Practice for Software Product Quality Evaluation and Certification: A Software Laboratory’s 25-Year Journey’. IEEE Software. 1-10. Available at: DOI:10.1109/MS.2024.3357119
