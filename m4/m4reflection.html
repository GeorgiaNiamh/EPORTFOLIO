Reflection

Software Engineering Project Management January 2025 reflection:

This essay shall reflect on my experience on during the module and how I feel it has influenced my practice going forward.
Learning:

Prior to this module, my understanding of project management was limited. I exposure to structured methodologies, especially within software engineering. The module introduced me to concepts such as cost estimation, project planning, and risk assessment. A particularly enlightening aspect was understanding why projects fail, as discussed by Agrawal et al. (2024).
The modules’ introduction to Gherkin statements (Behave, 2020) emphasised the value of clear, testable requirements, enhancing both alignment and product quality. This has reshaped my perspective on the importance of integrating theoretical knowledge with practical application.
The concept of the Halo Effect (Gu et al., 2023) was particularly thought-provoking, as it highlighted how users’ perceptions of one aspect of a software product, such as its visual design, can influence their overall experience and judgment of its functionality. This challenged me to rethink how I approach my own projects. Previously, I had focused more on technical accuracy and less on the user’s emotional or aesthetic response. The module encouraged me to reflect on the broader impact of design choices, prompting a shift in how I consider user experience as a whole.
Understanding the Halo Effect made me realise that even subtle elements, like interface design or branding, can shape users' expectations and satisfaction in ways I hadn’t previously appreciated.
Moving forward, I intend to incorporate structured planning techniques, such as detailed time estimations and risk assessments, into my future projects. This process has not only deepened my understanding of project management but also equipped me with practical strategies to enhance my professional practice.
Group work:

Coming into the group element of this module, I felt more prepared thanks to experience from a previous module. That prior exposure me to identify areas could be improved particularly around communication and coordination. With that in mind, I was more intentional in this group project, and it quickly became clear how vital clear communication is to a successful collaboration. As a group, we made a conscious decision early on to prioritise regular updates and transparency about our progress, which created a much more cohesive and efficient workflow. It was encouraging to see how something as simple as consistent communication could have such a positive impact on the work.
Unit 2’s focus on estimating, planning, and risk management was particularly helpful. These were areas I hadn’t always considered in depth before, especially in a group context. Applying those principles to our project helped ground our decisions and gave us a clearer sense of direction. It made me realise how often assumptions go unspoken in group work, and how effective planning helps to surface those early on.
Another key learning point was how we each brought different strengths to the table. I found myself taking on the Gherkin statements, as I had developed an interest in that area. This kind of skill-based task distribution improved the quality of the project overall. Reflecting on this, I now see the value of actively identifying both my own and others’ capabilities at the start of a project. It’s something I’ll aim to do more deliberately in future group work.
Presentation:

The module provided a solid foundation for preparing my presentation. Drawing on the work of Agrawal et al. (2024), I developed a clear plan that helped me identify and avoid pitfalls. Learning about the types of risks that can affect projects made me much more intentional in how I approached the task. I began by breaking the presentation into manageable tasks and mapping these onto a weekly timeline. This structure not only gave me clear direction but also created space to reflect on progress regularly. Inspired by the reading in Unit 3 (Anton & Nucu, 2020), I incorporated simple testing methods into my workflow, reviewing each task at the end of the week to ensure I was on track. This iterative approach made the overall process more manageable and helped me stay focused on my goals.
In the presentations content, I wanted to actively apply concepts from the module, particularly the Halo Effect (Gu et al., 2023). I focused on creating a cohesive and polished visual identity for my Synputer design, aiming to create a strong first impression and demonstrate how user perception can be influenced by design. This was something I had not considered before the module but will be taking forward.
In addition, I expanded my research into the broader market around the Synputer, which gave me valuable context and helped ground my presentation in real-world relevance. This step pushed me beyond the project brief and made me consider the Synputer not just as a theoretical product, but as something that would exist in a competitive environment.
Another area of development was my exploration of agile methodologies. Previously, my understanding had been surface-level, but in preparing for the presentation I looked more closely at different approaches. I found the concept of Extreme Programming (Hooda, 2023) particularly relevant. This method’s emphasis on adaptability and continuous feedback aligned well with the project’s needs and helped shape how I framed my approach.
Presenting itself was both daunting and rewarding. It pushed me to be more strategic, not just in what I presented, but in how I structured and delivered the content. I made a conscious effort to ensure each section of the presentation worked together to support a clear vision. In doing so, I saw just how important it is to have a strong direction behind a project. Without that, I don’t believe the presentation would have come together as effectively.
Looking back, this experience not only strengthened my confidence in managing a structured project but also helped me understand how to present complex ideas clearly and persuasively, skills I know will be invaluable in my future career.
Conclusion:

Through reflecting on this module, I can see the skills I have gained and how these skills will assist me going forward. I feel the module has allowed me insight into an area previously unknown to me and this will undoubtedly assist me going forward with my programming and project management skills.
