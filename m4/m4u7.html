Response to the CUE model:


As a Project Manager, it's crucial to recognise that users' emotional responses are a key ingredient for a successful experience. The CUE model (Van der Linden et al., 2019) highlights that these feelings – like joy or frustration – along with physical reactions and how users behave, are central to their overall UX. These emotions significantly shape how users perceive our product, how satisfied they are, and whether they'll remain loyal.
One effective approach is to proactively consider emotions early in the design process. Desmet and Hekkert (2007) explain that our feelings are triggered by how we evaluate a product's qualities in relation to what matters to us. This suggests that involving users in collaborative design sessions and using empathy mapping can help us uncover their underlying emotional needs and expectations, which might not be obvious from just looking at what the product needs to do. By understanding these emotional drivers, we can design experiences that resonate more deeply.
Furthermore, we can use iterative usability testing as a way to monitor emotional responses throughout development. Bargas-Avila and Hornbæk (2011) point out that UX is complex, and emotions are influenced by both how easy something is to use and how it looks and feels. By observing users' facial expressions, listening to their comments, and noting their actions during testing, project teams can identify emotional pain points and address them before the final release. This helps us refine the experience to be not just functional, but also enjoyable.
Finally, it's important to have ongoing ways to get feedback after the product launches. Hassenzahl and Tractinsky (2006) remind us that user experience isn't static; people's feelings can change over time. Feedback channels like satisfaction surveys, the Net Promoter Score (NPS), or even analysing sentiment within user comments can provide real-time insights into user emotions. This allows the project team to continuously improve the product based on how users are actually feeling about it.
By integrating an understanding of user emotions throughout the entire lifecycle – from initial design to post-launch improvements – we don't just increase user satisfaction. We also build stronger trust and encourage long-term engagement. In today's competitive market, paying attention to how users feel can be just as vital as ensuring the product functions correctly.

    First impression has a big impact of user’s opinion and experiences. User have a subconscious bias based on their previous user experiences. This is something compounded by the halo effect. In the article (Gu et al, 2023) focuses heavily on the aesthetic of websites and applications and how this influences users’ opinion.
    In today’s world where the idea of perceived trust can also be seen through users being influenced by others. In particular the idea of perceived trust from a notable figure or celebrity when it came to products. This then plays into the brand image and thus adds to the halo effect (Hivo, 2023). This can be seen with websites and app as sites often chose celebrity spokes people to represent the brand. A recent example would be the celebrity of Elon Musk now being attached to the brand X (formerly Twitter) and his influence on how consumers view the site either positively or negatively.
